# Conclusions

1. **SWINGERsim reproduces the three published systems.** All are stable in every test, and all 400–600
   members of each ensemble stay intact forward and backward in time.
2. **Light curves.** After the period and phase of each pair are anchored to the minima, the synthetic TESS
   light curves agree with the observations to 2–4 ppt in the best sectors, about one per cent of the eclipse
   depths (V994 Her after removing a 0.663-d oscillation). The depths are those of the published stars
   multiplied by one free scale factor per sector, which absorbs extra light in the aperture: 0.98 for
   BU CMi and 0.97–0.98 for the SPOC sectors of TIC 120362137, but 0.46–0.51 for the uncorrected
   light-curve windows of TIC 120362137 (a bright neighbour) and 1.2–2.0 for V994 Her.
3. **Eclipse timings.** SWINGERsim reproduces the complex O − C diagrams (apsidal motion, light-travel time,
   dynamical ETVs) of all six eclipsing pairs, to 1.2–1.7 min for TIC 120362137, as well as or better than the
   published model on the same minima, and to the scatter of the data elsewhere.
4. **Joint fit.** With an extra error for every source of minima, priors where the data are weak, radial
   velocities, light curves with correlated noise, and MCMC, the fit reproduces the published masses to
   1–5 % and inclinations to 0.15° in BU CMi and TIC 120362137.
5. **Three differences, traced as far as the papers allow.** BU CMi's outer eccentricity differs by 2.6σ
   when SWINGERsim includes the classical apsidal advance; with point masses the fit agrees, and the
   published apsidal periods match SWINGERsim's point-mass integration to 1 %. The paper estimates a
   tidal advance of the same size but does not say which tidal term its integration used, so the cause
   remains open; freeing the apsidal constants does not remove it (the minima then want
   $k_2 \le 0.0023$ for pair A against 0.0042 of the models). TIC 120362137's published apsidal period is a theoretical value. V994 Her's classical
   apsidal rates were fitted freely in the paper and differ from stellar structure; for pair B the
   authors found the same themselves (k₂ about 0.83 times the theoretical value), the large difference
   is pair C, which a companion of 0.13–0.31 M☉ on an 80–182-d orbit could explain.
6. **Data that need a second look:** the radial velocities of BU CMi (17–25 km/s scatter), the A–B
   eccentricity of V994 Her (0.51–0.59 from the minima against 0.69), and the depths of TESS sector 26 of
   V994 Her in the SPOC photometry (probably the crowding correction).
