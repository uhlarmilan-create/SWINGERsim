# Conclusions

1. **V907 Sco:** SWINGERsim reproduces the switching eclipses from the dynamics alone. The third star
   (0.98 (+0.05/−0.04) M☉, 142.09 ± 0.04 d), the mutual inclination (25.8°) and the nodal period (65.5 yr)
   agree with Zasche et al. (2023). The model switches the eclipses off and on when they were observed
   to do so, predicts the 2021–2023 eclipse depths to within 5 %, and predicts the end of the present
   eclipses in 2052–2057.
2. **V907 Sco:** the radial velocities of 1983–1994 fit the 142-d orbit; the 99-d outer period is
   ruled out. The eclipse times are dominated by the dynamical effect of the third star.
3. **QX Cas:** a third star of 0.5–4 M☉ at 0.5–3 years turns the binary at the observed rate and
   switched off its eclipses in the 1990s; the inclination must have fallen at ≥ 0.5°/yr after 1980.
4. **QX Cas is detected in TESS without eclipses**, at the orbital period and its harmonics, with the
   amplitude SWINGERsim predicts.
5. **QX Cas, open:** the photographic minima of 1933–1942 need an inclination of ≥ 76–78° then. A fit
   that enforces this exists, but then the eclipses stay detectable until 1999.0, against the absence of
   eclipses in the mid-1990s; the old eclipse depths also need third light that a single value cannot
   supply. Both point to the adopted stellar radii and light ratio. An MCMC of that fit, conditioned on
   the whole inclination history, puts the end of the geometric eclipses in 2000.0–2006.5 (16–84 %) but
   leaves their return open (2072–2130, 4 % of the samples not before 2150). A joint fit of the depths
   and the TESS light curve and the DASCH plates are the next steps.
6. **AH Cep:** 125 years of eclipse times give a third body of ≥ 6.6 M☉ on a 70-yr orbit. It cannot
   change the inclination measurably; a fourth body is not needed, and the proposed 9-yr fourth body
   would be unstable. TESS suggests i ≈ 72.5° with about 5 % third light, which is in tension with the
   published inclination and the spectra and requires an independent light-curve analysis.
7. **Gaia DR3:** none of the three has a RUWE above 1.4. For V907 Sco this is a tension: the modelled
   light-centre motion of about 0.3 mas on the 142-d orbit should usually be visible; Gaia DR4 epoch
   astrometry can test it directly. For QX Cas the parallax changed by 4.2σ between DR2 and DR3, a hint
   but not evidence of orbital motion; for AH Cep the Hipparcos–Gaia acceleration remains to be checked.
